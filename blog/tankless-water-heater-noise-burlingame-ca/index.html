Why Is My Tankless Water Heater Making Noise?

Most tankless heater noise comes from scale on the heat exchanger, water flow that is too low or too high, or a loose vent or mount. A rumble or crackle usually means scale and a flush fixes it. A bang when you close a tap is water hammer, not the heater.

Here is how to tell the sounds apart and what to do about each.

Normal Sounds Versus Problem Sounds

Tankless units make some noise. A fan humming, a click on ignition and a soft whoosh from the burner are normal. Sounds that are new, louder or repeated deserve a look.

Scale and Rumbling

When water heats in a coated heat exchanger, it boils in tiny pockets and makes a rumbling or crackling sound. Scale also reduces heat transfer and wastes gas, so quieting the noise often improves efficiency.

Burlingame water is very soft, with hardness averaging 32 mg/L in the city's 2022 report, so scale forms slowly here. If your unit is noisy, it is worth checking other causes first, but a yearly flush is still wise because even small deposits add up over years.

How to Flush a Tankless Heater

Shut off power, gas and water

Turn off the unit and close the isolation valves.

Connect hoses

Attach a hose from the service port to a pump, and another from the other port to a bucket.

Circulate descaler

Run a descaling solution or white vinegar through the unit for the time the manual specifies, often about an hour.

Rinse and restore

Flush with clean water, clean the inlet filter and reopen valves and power.

Without service valves, a plumber should add them. The installation is simple and pays off with every flush.

Water Hammer and High Pressure

A bang or shudder when a tap or appliance closes quickly is water hammer. It stresses pipes and fittings and can loosen connections. Pressure that is too high makes it worse.

Burlingame runs pressure-reducing valves and booster pumps in the hills, and pressure varies by neighborhood. We measure at the hose bib and add a regulator or a hammer arrestor where it helps. A tankless heater can add its own noise if the supply pressure is high.

Vent and Mounting Issues

A loose vent pipe can rattle in the wind, and a unit that is not firmly mounted can vibrate. Both are simple to fix, though vent work on a gas unit should be done by a professional because it carries exhaust.

If you hear a hiss or smell gas, leave the house and call 911 and then the gas utility.

Noise Related to the Installation

Some noise comes from how the unit was installed and not from the heater itself. Pipes that touch framing without padding carry vibration, and copper lines that are strapped tightly can tick as they expand. A plumber can add cushioned straps and isolate the lines from the studs.

The vent and the wall also matter. A vent that is too long or has too many bends can whistle as exhaust passes through it. A gas line that is undersized can make the burner sputter. If the noise started right after installation, tell the installer, since fixing it is a quick adjustment in most cases.

Units mounted on a thin wall can amplify sound into the next room. Mounting on a solid wall with a backing board reduces it, and we consider that when we plan an install.

A Maintenance Calendar That Keeps Noise Down

  • Clean the inlet filter every few months, especially after plumbing work in the house.
  • Flush the heat exchanger once a year.
  • Check the vent termination for nests and debris each fall.
  • Test the pressure at a hose bib once a year, since high pressure adds noise.
  • Have a technician check the burner and gas pressure every couple of years.

A tankless heater that is kept clean runs quieter and lasts longer. Many noise complaints disappear after a simple flush and filter cleaning.

If your unit is on a recirculation loop, check the pump too. A pump that is wearing out can hum or rattle and is easy to mistake for the heater.

When Noise Means Call a Professional

Some sounds should not wait for a weekend project. A loud bang from the burner at ignition, a hiss with a gas smell, repeated clicking with no flame or a screech from the fan all point to a fault inside the unit. Turn the heater off at the control and call a plumber.

A technician can read stored error codes, test the igniter and fan and check combustion. Fixing a fan or igniter early prevents a lockout during a cold morning. Call (650) 860-1670 and we will schedule a visit or advise by phone.

Safety Steps for a Gas Tankless Unit

  • If you smell gas, leave the house at once and call 911, then the gas utility.
  • Do not flip switches, use a phone inside or relight the unit?
  • Turn the unit off at its control if you hear loud banging or a hiss.
  • Keep the area around the vent clear of stored items and debris.
  • Let a licensed plumber inspect the burner and vent before restarting.

Venting and gas supply requirements come from the manufacturer's instructions and the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
