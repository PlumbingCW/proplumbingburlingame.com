About Ray Park

Real estate guides place Ray Park in north Burlingame near the ballfields and dog park, with Lincoln Elementary next door and the Burlingame Plaza shopping center nearby. They describe 1950s and 1960s ranch homes, many updated, and some that gained second stories over time.

We did not find an official boundary map, so we describe the area by those anchors. Every house in a mid-century neighborhood is different, which is why we diagnose before we recommend.

Homes That Have Grown

A ranch house that gains a second story or an extra bathroom often has its plumbing extended in stages. New drains tie into old ones, and a water heater sized for the original house may now serve more fixtures.

When the hot water runs out or pressure drops with several fixtures on, it is worth asking whether the heater and the supply pipe still match the house. A water heater repair visit can tell you whether the tank is the problem, and repiping is an option when the supply lines are the limit.

Permits and Water Heater Replacement

Replacing a water heater in Burlingame needs a plumbing permit from the Building Division, and California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires the heater to be braced or strapped. We handle both through water heater installation.

A new tank is a good time to look at the expansion tank, the relief valve and the shutoff, which protect the heater and the house. Installations follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

What Burlingame Water Means for Fixtures

Very soft water

Burlingame's water comes from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission and averaged 32 mg/L hardness in the 2022 report.

That is very soft, so mineral scale is not the usual cause of a slow faucet. Worn cartridges and washers are more likely, and a toilet repair or faucet visit is often a simple fix.
