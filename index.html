Plumbing in Burlingame

What local homes ask of their plumbing

Burlingame is a mix of older homes near Burlingame Avenue, Broadway and the Easton Addition and newer construction on the hills toward Skyline Boulevard. The age of the house decides most of what goes wrong. Homes built before the mid-1960s were not required to have a cleanout at the right-of-way, so a lateral that backs up can be harder to reach and clear in those houses.

The water itself is gentle on pipes. Burlingame buys all of its supply from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission, mostly Hetch Hetchy snowmelt, and the city's 2022 water quality report puts hardness at an average of 32 mg/L. That means little scale inside water heaters and faucets, so we look first at pressure, pipe age and tree roots when something fails.

Pressure varies across town. The city runs booster pump stations and pressure-reducing valves for the areas above 120 feet in elevation, and a home on a hill can see very different pressure from one in the flats near El Camino Real. A plumber who checks pressure with a gauge before replacing a fixture saves you from repeating the repair. Our leak detection and water line repair work both start with that reading.

Selling or buying also brings plumbing into the picture. Burlingame requires a sewer lateral test when a property with buildings more than 25 years old is sold or transferred, and repairs have to be approved before title moves. We run a sewer camera inspection first so you know what the city's test is likely to find.

All of our work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and permits come from the Burlingame Building Division when the job requires one.
