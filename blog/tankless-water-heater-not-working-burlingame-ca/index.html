Tankless Water Heater Not Working: What to Check

A tankless heater that stops producing hot water usually has one of five problems: no gas or power, a blocked vent, a clogged inlet filter, a failed sensor or a scaled heat exchanger. The error code on the display points to the cause in most cases.

Start with the basics below, because several fixes take a few minutes and require no tools. If you smell gas at any point, leave the house and call 911 and then the gas utility.

First Checks Anyone Can Do

Confirm the unit has power and gas. Look at the display, and make sure the breaker or outlet is live and the gas shutoff on the line to the heater is open. A tripped breaker or a closed valve explains a lot of cold showers.

Open a hot tap fully. Many tankless units need a minimum flow, often around half a gallon a minute, to switch on. If the tap barely runs, the unit does not fire, and a clogged aerator can be the whole problem.

  • Display on and showing a code, or dark.
  • Gas valve handle in line with the pipe.
  • Breaker on and no tripped GFCI nearby.
  • Hot tap fully open and flowing well.
  • Cold water valve to the heater open.

Reading Common Error Codes

Codes vary by brand, so check your manual. The ideas behind them are similar, and the table shows what they usually mean.

Cold Water Sandwich and Short Cycling

A burst of hot, then cold, then hot water is the cold water sandwich. It happens when you turn off a tap briefly and the water in the pipe cools. It is a feature of tankless systems, not a fault, and a small buffer tank or a recirculation setup reduces it.

A unit that cycles on and off at low flow may be asked to heat too little water. Opening the tap more, or using a faucet with a higher flow, usually helps.

Flushing the Heat Exchanger

Minerals build up inside the heat exchanger and slow the unit down. Burlingame water is very soft, with average hardness near 32 mg/L in the city's 2022 report, so scale grows slowly here, but a yearly flush is still a good habit. If your unit was installed with service valves, you can flush it with a small pump and a bucket of white vinegar or a descaling solution as the manual directs.

Without service valves, the flush needs a plumber to install them first. The installation is a short job and makes every future flush easier.

Vent and Gas Problems Need a Professional

A blocked vent or a gas supply that cannot keep up with the burner is a safety issue. Carbon monoxide leaves the house through the vent, and a unit that shuts down on an exhaust code is protecting you.

We check vent length and pitch, gas line size and pressure at the unit while it is firing. Gas line size matters because a tankless burner draws far more gas than a tank, and a line sized for the old tank often falls short.

Gas Supply and Venting Checks

Tankless burners draw several times the gas a tank heater does, and a supply line sized for the tank may be too small. Symptoms include a flame that lights and then goes out, the unit shutting down at high demand and error codes related to ignition. A gas pressure test while the unit is firing shows whether the line keeps up.

The vent is a safety path and must stay clear. Nests, snow in cold climates, lint from a dryer vent installed close by and a crushed pipe can all cause exhaust errors. Inspect the outside termination for blockage, and do not run the unit while the vent is damaged.

If you smell gas or exhaust, leave the house and call 911 and then the gas utility, and do not try to relight or reset the unit.

What a Service Visit Includes

  • Reading the error code history from the control board.
  • Checking gas pressure and burner flame.
  • Cleaning the inlet filter and the flame sensor.
  • Testing flow, temperature and the thermistors.
  • Flushing the heat exchanger and checking for leaks.
  • Inspecting the vent and condensate drain.

This takes a few hours for a straightforward unit. We explain what we found and what it costs before replacing anything. If a heat exchanger is cracked or leaking, repair is rarely worthwhile and we discuss replacement options.

Safety Steps for a Gas Tankless Unit

  • If you smell gas, leave the house at once and call 911, then the gas utility.
  • Do not keep resetting a unit that locks out on an ignition or exhaust code?
  • Never remove the vent or the front cover while the unit is on.
  • Keep the area around the vent clear of stored items and debris.
  • Let a licensed plumber test the gas pressure and vent before you use it again.

Venting and gas supply requirements come from the manufacturer's instructions and the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
