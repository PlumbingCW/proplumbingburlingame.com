How to Detect an Underground Water Pipe Leak

The fastest way to detect an underground water pipe leak is the meter test. Turn off every tap and appliance, read the meter, wait 15 minutes without using water and read it again. If the numbers change, water is leaking somewhere between the meter and your fixtures.

This guide explains the test, the yard clues that show where the leak sits and when to bring in a plumber with listening equipment.

By the Burlingame Pro Plumbing team | Updated November 16, 2026

The Meter Test, Step by Step

Stop all water use

Turn off taps, the dishwasher, the washer, ice makers and irrigation. Check that no toilet is running.

Find and read the meter

The meter sits in a box near the street or the property line. Lift the lid carefully and wipe the glass.

Look at the leak indicator

Many meters have a small triangle or dial that spins when any water moves.

Wait and compare

Write down the reading, wait 15 minutes with no use, then read again. A change means a leak.

If the dial moves, close the main shutoff to the house and watch again. If it stops, the leak is inside your system. If it keeps turning, the leak is between the meter and the shutoff, which means the buried line.

Clues in the Yard and the House

Soggy ground on a dry day

A patch that stays wet or a spot where grass grows greener than the rest can mark a buried leak.

Sinking pavement or a soft driveway edge

Water washing out soil leaves a void under the surface.

Sound of water in the pipes at night

A faint hiss with everything off is a pressurized line leaking.

Pressure that has fallen

A leak upstream of fixtures robs flow across the house.

Leaking water does not always surface. In sandy or well-draining soil it can run down for months before you see a wet spot, and the bill may be the first sign.

What a High Bill Does and Does Not Tell You

A sudden jump is a clue, not proof. Irrigation settings, guests, a running toilet and seasonal use all affect it. Compare the same billing period from last year and look at the daily usage if the city provides it.

A leak shows as a steady flow at all hours, including overnight. If the usage is flat at a constant rate while you were away, that supports a leak. For help reading the numbers, see our leak detection page.

Who Owns the Pipe in Burlingame

The City of Burlingame buys its water from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission, and the Water Division maintains the public mains and the service up to the meter. The line from the meter to your house is private and is your responsibility.

If the leak is in the street or before the meter, the city is the one to call, and its Water Division takes calls on weekdays with after-hours dispatch for urgent main breaks. If it is after the meter, a plumber handles it.

How Plumbers Pinpoint a Buried Leak

We isolate the line, listen for the sound of escaping water with acoustic sensors and trace the pipe route with a locator. A pressure test confirms the leak and narrows the section. Then we mark the spot and explain the repair options.

The goal is a small excavation at the leak, not a trench of guesswork. Once the spot is confirmed, the repair is a water line repair and, if the line is old throughout, we discuss replacing the whole run.

Mistakes That Waste Time and Money

  • Digging where you think the leak is before confirming the location.
  • Ignoring a high bill because the yard looks dry.
  • Assuming the city is responsible without checking the meter side.
  • Adding sealant to a pipe that has failed along its length.
  • Waiting until the sinkhole forms to call.

A leak is easier and cheaper to repair at the first sign. Water that runs under pavement can wash out soil and cause settling that is expensive to fix. The meter test takes a quarter of an hour and tells you whether to call.

Keep records. Photos of the meter, the bill and any wet spots help the plumber and any claim you make later.

What We Bring to a Leak Search

A plumber who searches for a buried leak carries a few tools you do not have at home. A ground microphone picks up the sound of water leaking from a pressurized pipe. A pipe locator traces the route of a line under soil or concrete. Pressure gauges and isolation caps test sections of pipe and show where the pressure drops.

Using these together, the search narrows from the whole property to a spot a couple of feet wide. That precision is what keeps the excavation small. It also tells us whether the pipe has one failure or many, which decides between a spot repair and replacing the run.

Once we have found it, we tell you the price of the repair before we start, and we explain whether the line should be replaced.

How Plumbers Confirm and Repair It to Code

After the leak is located, the repair follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). New pipe must be rated for buried drinking water use, joints must be pressure tested and the work may need a permit. Testing the line before the trench is closed is the step that separates a repair that lasts from one that leaks again.

If the Leak Is Under the House

Not every leak is in the yard. A line running under the foundation can leak into the slab or crawlspace, and the signs are warm floors, damp spots and sounds from the floor. A slab leak repair follows a different process, and a crawlspace is reached from below.
