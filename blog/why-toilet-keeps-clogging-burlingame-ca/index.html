Why Does My Toilet Keep Clogging?

A toilet that clogs repeatedly has one of four problems: too much paper or wipes, a weak flush, a partial blockage in the trap or a restriction in the drain line behind it. The test is simple. If only the toilet is affected, the cause is probably the toilet. If other fixtures gurgle or slow, the line is the problem.

Here is how to sort it out and what to do for each cause.

Start by Asking What Else Is Slow

Run the bathroom sink and the tub after a clog and see if they drain well. A tub that gurgles when the toilet flushes, or a toilet that bubbles when the shower drains, points to the shared drain line.

If only the toilet misbehaves, focus on the toilet. If several fixtures show symptoms, treat it as a line problem and see drain cleaning or, if it affects the whole house, sewer line cleaning.

Things That Cause Clogs

  • Wipes labeled flushable, which do not break down like paper.
  • Paper towels, tissues and cotton products.
  • Feminine hygiene products and diapers.
  • Excess toilet paper in one flush.
  • Toys and small objects.

Even with a good toilet, wipes catch on roughness in the pipe and build into a mass. Burlingame's city sewer system and your own lateral both suffer from them, and they are a leading cause of backups in many cities.

Drain and Vent Problems Behind the Toilet

A toilet needs air in the drain line to flush well. A blocked plumbing vent on the roof can cause slow drains and gurgling. A partial blockage in the branch line from the toilet to the main line behaves the same way.

A lateral with roots or a belly will back up repeatedly, even if each backup clears with a plunger. Older Burlingame homes built before the mid-1960s were not required to have a right-of-way cleanout, and many have original clay laterals. A sewer camera inspection shows what is going on.

How to Clear a Clog Safely

Stop flushing

A second flush with a blocked drain pushes water over the rim.

Use a flange plunger

Seal it over the drain and push steadily, then pull back. Repeat several times.

Try a closet auger

Feed the cable through the trap and crank to break up or retrieve the clog.

Avoid chemical openers

They rarely clear a real clog, they can damage fittings and they make later work hazardous.

If the water level rises and does not fall after plunging, stop and call. Forcing it can overflow the bowl.

The Role of Toilet Paper and Water Level

Not all toilet paper dissolves equally. Thick, quilted or ultra-plush paper takes longer to break down than thinner paper, and using a large wad in one flush overwhelms a weak toilet. If clogs happen mostly after heavy use, try fewer sheets per flush or a double flush.

The water level in the tank also matters. A level set too low reduces the force of the flush, and a flapper that closes early cuts the flush short. Check the fill line marked inside the tank and adjust the float. A cheap fix can end a long run of clogs.

In a house with a low-flow toilet that was installed with an old drain, the weak flush can leave solids in the line to settle. Over time that adds up to a blockage in the branch drain.

When a Repeated Clog Signals a Line Problem

A toilet that clogs once in a while is normal. A toilet that clogs every week, especially after being augered, may be the first sign of a partly blocked drain behind it. Roots, a sag in the pipe or a rough joint catch paper and waste.

A camera inspection shows what is happening in the line. If the toilet is on a branch that joins the main under the house, the problem could be a short section that is easy to fix. If the main itself is rooted, the fix is bigger. Call (650) 860-1670 and we will look.

Ignoring it risks a full backup at a bad time, such as a holiday gathering or while you are away. Fixing the cause early is cheaper than an emergency visit.
