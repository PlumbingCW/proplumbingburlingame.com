Is a Tankless Heater Right for Your Home

Tankless units suit households that want endless hot water in less space. They are a poor fit when the gas line is undersized or the vent route is difficult. The savings depend on how much hot water you use, and they are real but not dramatic for a small household.

Tankless advantages

  • Hot water as long as the flow rate is met.
  • Wall-mounted, freeing floor space.
  • No standby tank to heat all day.
  • Often longer service life with care.

Tankless tradeoffs

  • Higher upfront cost than a standard tank.
  • Gas line and vent may need upgrading.
  • Flow limits when many fixtures run.
  • Needs yearly flushing in many homes.

What We Check Before Recommending One

  • Gas line size and run length, since tankless burners draw a lot of gas at once.
  • Vent routing and termination to a safe point.
  • Electrical outlet for the controls.
  • Peak demand, such as two showers and a dishwasher together.
  • Water pressure and incoming temperature.
  • Whether the new unit meets Bay Area air rules for gas appliances.

The Air District delayed its zero-NOx standard for gas water heaters to January 2028 in 2026, and it applies at the time of purchase. We explain how that affects a gas tankless choice for your timeline, and we compare it with a heat pump option on the water heater replacement page.

Burlingame Water and a Tankless Unit

Burlingame water is very soft, with an average hardness of 32 mg/L in the city's 2022 report. In hard-water cities, scale inside a tankless heat exchanger is the main failure. Here it builds slowly, so flushing is a maintenance habit more than an urgent chore.

The water is chloraminated and fairly alkaline, which can affect rubber parts and taste, but it does not harm a heat exchanger. We still recommend an annual check and flush, particularly for units on a hot recirculation loop.

Servicing an Existing Tankless Unit

Error codes, cold water sandwiches and a unit that cycles on and off are the common calls. Usually the cause is a clogged inlet screen, a failing flow sensor, a vent problem or a gas supply issue.

We flush the heat exchanger, clean filters, test the sensors and check the vent and gas pressure. If the unit is beyond repair, we say so and discuss whether to replace it with another tankless model or a tank. For a more basic failure on a tank unit, see water heater repair.

Recirculation and Hot Water Wait Times

A tankless heater does not remove the wait for hot water to travel from the heater to the tap. A long run to a far bathroom still takes time. A recirculating pump or a small buffer tank solves that, and some units have the feature built in.

We ask how long the run is to your farthest fixture and design around it. Recirculation adds a little cost and a little energy use, so we recommend it only where the wait is a real annoyance.
