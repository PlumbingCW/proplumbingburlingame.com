When a Line Needs Jetting Now

Jetting is often planned, but a line packed with grease or roots can also be an emergency. Restaurants, rental buildings and homes with repeated backups benefit most, because the problem is the coating on the pipe and not a single clog.

Cutting regrows quickly. Jetting removes more of the root mass and fine hairs.

Slow drains across the building

How a Hydro Jet Works

A hose with a nozzle feeds into the line, and the nozzle sprays water in a pattern that scrubs the pipe in a full circle. The forward jets pull the hose through while the rear jets push debris back to the cleanout. A cable pushes through a clog. A jet washes the pipe.

We start with a camera so we know what material the pipe is. Fragile or broken pipe is not jetted, since pressure on a damaged section can make it worse.

Jetting Compared With Cabling

Cable or auger

  • Opens a hole through the clog.
  • Leaves the coating on the pipe walls.
  • Faster for a single soft blockage.
  • Fits most small drains.

Hydro jetting

  • Scours grease, scale and roots from the walls.
  • Restores the pipe to near full diameter.
  • Better for lines that keep closing.
  • Best for main lines and kitchens.

Where Jetting Helps Burlingame Properties

Burlingame's water is soft, so scale is a smaller issue than in hard-water cities, but grease and roots are not. Kitchen lines for restaurants along Burlingame Avenue and Broadway collect fats from daily use, and a regular jetting schedule keeps them flowing between grease trap services.

Older residential laterals with root intrusion also respond well, as long as the camera shows the pipe is intact. If it is cracked or collapsed, a sewer line repair is the next step. For a one-time stoppage, drain cleaning with a cable is often enough.

Commercial kitchens get a maintenance schedule from our commercial plumbing team so a backup never hits during dinner service.

The cleanouts that make jetting possible are a feature of the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and an accessible one near the building speeds the job and lowers the cost.

Safety

What to Do Before a Jetting Visit

  • Clear access to the cleanout and mark where it is.
  • Tell us if the line has been repaired, lined or collapsed in the past.
  • Keep people and pets away from the cleanout area during the work.
  • Do not pour chemical cleaners into the line beforehand?
  • Plan for the sink or toilets on that line to be out of use for a short time.

Chemicals left in a line can splash back when the jet starts, so we ask you to leave the line as it is.

Limits

Safety and Limits of Jetting

Jetting is safe on sound pipe and not suitable for every line. We will tell you honestly if the camera shows that the pipe is too fragile. Collapsed, severely offset or broken lines need repair before cleaning makes sense.

Flush valves, toilets and fixtures inside the home can be damaged by pressure, so we jet from the cleanout, not through household fixtures.
