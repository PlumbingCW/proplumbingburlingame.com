Burlingame, Our Home Base

Burlingame, ZIP code 94010, is where we start the truck. The city is known for its Burlingame Avenue shopping district, the Broadway district and neighborhoods that range from older streets near downtown to hillside areas toward Skyline Boulevard.

Plumbing here follows the age of the house. Homes built before the mid-1960s were not required to have a right-of-way cleanout, so older sewer laterals can be harder to clear and inspect. Newer hillside homes deal with pressure differences, since the city uses booster pumps and pressure-reducing valves above 120 feet in elevation.

Our drain cleaning, leak detection and water heater repair teams handle most local calls, and commercial plumbing covers the downtown shops and restaurants.

What Burlingame Owners Should Know

Water, sewer and permits

Burlingame buys all its water from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission, and the city reports very soft water, with average hardness of 32 mg/L in its 2022 report. The city maintains the sewer from the right-of-way cleanout to the main, and the lateral on your side is yours.

A sewer lateral test is required when a property with buildings older than 25 years is sold. Plumbing permits come from the Building Division, and permit fees are waived when a licensed contractor replaces a gas water heater or furnace with a heat pump.

If you are selling, our sewer camera inspection shows what the city test may find.

Why Local Rules Change the Job

Every city sets its own rules for sewer laterals, permits and water service. Burlingame requires a lateral test at sale for older buildings, and San Mateo has its own inspection program with different triggers and exemptions. A plumber who works in both knows which rules apply to your address and which forms the city will ask for.

Water supply differs as well. Burlingame is served by its own city water system that buys from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission, while San Mateo is served by Cal Water's Bayshore district, which also buys from the SFPUC. The result is similar source water with different hardness numbers and different utilities to call about a main break.

Knowing who owns the pipe matters in an emergency. A leak before the meter is the utility's, and a leak after it is yours, and a backup in the public main is the city's while one in your lateral is yours. We help you work out which it is, so you make one phone call and not three.
