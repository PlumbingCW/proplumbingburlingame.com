A Blocked Main Line Is an Emergency

A branch clog affects one fixture. A main line blockage affects the house. The telltale pattern is a toilet that bubbles when the shower drains, or water rising in the lowest tub or floor drain when the washer runs.

If you see that, stop using water. Every gallon you add has to go somewhere, and in a blocked line that means onto your floor. Call us, then keep everyone away from the affected drain.

  • Several fixtures slow or back up at the same time.
  • Sewage or gray water at a floor drain or the lowest tub.
  • Gurgling from toilets and drains with a sewer smell.
  • A cleanout in the yard that is full or overflowing.

What Causes Main Line Stoppages Here

Roots lead the list in Burlingame. The city has long streets lined with mature trees, and tree roots seek out the moisture at pipe joints. Clay and older concrete laterals have joints every few feet, and a hair-thin root can grow into a mass that catches wipes and grease.

Age matters. Buildings from before the mid-1960s were not required to have a right-of-way cleanout, so access to the line may be limited and the pipe itself may be original clay or cast iron.

Grease is the other regular culprit, especially for restaurants and homes with heavy cooking. If your line has been cleared before and closes again, hydro jetting scours the pipe walls in a way a cable cannot.

How We Clear and Confirm the Line

Locate the cleanout

We work from the right-of-way cleanout if there is one, or from a vent, a removed toilet or the house cleanout.

Cut the roots or break the clog

A heavy cable with a cutting head opens the line. We recover debris where we can.

Run water and test flow

We confirm the line drains freely and that fixtures no longer back up.

Camera the lateral

We look at the pipe for roots, cracks, offsets or a belly, and show you what is there.

Clearing a line without looking is a guess. The camera tells you whether the line will stay open or whether the next backup is coming.

The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), calls for cleanouts that stay accessible along a drain line, and a property without one is a candidate for adding one.

Public Sewer or Private Lateral

Who clears what

The City of Burlingame maintains the sewer main and the segment from the right-of-way cleanout to the main. Your lateral, from that cleanout back to the house, is the property owner's.

The city cannot relieve a backup on private property. If your neighbors are also backing up, report it to Public Works, because the problem may be in the main. If only your home is affected, the stoppage is on your lateral.

Aftercare

Keeping the Line Open

After a cleaning, small habits keep the line clear. Wipes labeled flushable are not, grease belongs in a can and not down the drain, and food scraps belong in the bin. If a tree sits over the lateral, plan a camera check each year or two.

A cleanout at the right-of-way is the best investment for future access. If your home lacks one, we can quote adding it with the permit.
