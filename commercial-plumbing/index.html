Plumbing Failures That Close a Business

A residential backup is a disruption. A commercial one can be a health code problem. Restaurants cannot serve food with a failed handwash sink, a floor drain backing up or no hot water, and retailers lose customers when restrooms close.

Burlingame Avenue, Broadway and Beyond

Burlingame's downtown along Burlingame Avenue and the Broadway district are full of restaurants, salons, shops and offices in buildings of many ages. Older buildings have older drains and shared lines, and a backup in one unit can affect the next.

Ground floor businesses often sit on a lateral shared with the building. Knowing where your line joins the building line, and who is responsible for each stretch, decides who calls us and who pays, and we help sort that out when a lease is unclear.

Because the city maintains the sewer only to the right-of-way cleanout, a blockage inside property lines is the building owner's to clear. We locate the stoppage quickly so the right party is involved.

Commercial fixtures, flush valves and backflow devices fall under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the Burlingame Building Division inspects larger work against it.

Safety

What to Do Until We Arrive

  • Close the main or the affected fixture shutoff and block off the wet area.
  • Move customers and staff away from standing water or sewage.
  • Turn off power to equipment sitting in water.
  • Put out wet floor signs and lay absorbent mats.
  • Note the time and photograph the damage for your insurer.
  • Tell the building manager if the problem could affect other tenants.

For a sewage backup in a food business, stop food prep in the affected area and follow the county health department's guidance on reopening.

Scheduling Work Around Your Hours

We schedule non-urgent work before opening, after closing or on slow days, and we stage repairs so the business is not down longer than necessary. For an emergency, we focus on getting you operational first, then schedule the permanent fix.

Recurring work, such as jetting a kitchen line or flushing a heater, is booked on a calendar so it does not arrive as an emergency.

Restaurants and Food Service

Kitchens put plumbing under stress that offices never see. Grease enters the lines daily, dish machines need steady hot water, floor drains take mop water and prep sinks run all day. A line that is a little slow on Tuesday becomes a backup on Saturday night.

We camera and jet kitchen lines on a schedule, check water heater capacity against your peak and test handwash sinks and floor drains so they work when the health inspector looks. If a drain backs up mid-service, we clear it first and then find the cause.

Restaurant owners also ask about flush valves, mop sinks and backflow devices. We repair and replace these, and we tell you plainly when a fixture is past saving.

Preventive Service for Properties

Property managers and owners of multi-unit buildings get the most from planned visits. A camera look at shared laterals, a pressure check at the meter and a review of water heaters and shutoff valves catches small problems before they become a tenant complaint.

We document each visit so you have a record, and we flag anything that needs the building owner's attention. This keeps emergencies rare and gives you a list to budget from.
