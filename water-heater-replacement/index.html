When Replacement Is the Only Answer

A leak from the bottom of the tank means the steel has corroded through. No patch holds, and the water will keep coming. If the heater is also old and the floor is wet, this is the point to replace and not repair.

Water pooling from the tank body

This is not a fitting leak. The tank has failed.

Rusty hot water at every tap

Corrosion inside the tank means the liner is going.

Heater well beyond its normal life

A tank that has run many years is likely to fail without warning.

Repeated repairs in a short time

Parts keep failing because the unit is wearing out.

Turn off the cold supply, switch off power or set the gas to pilot and call. We handle the rest.

California Strapping and Burlingame Permits

Rules that apply to a replacement

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires new and replacement residential water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped to resist falling or sliding in an earthquake. It also applies to existing residential heaters, and it covers standard tanks up to 120 gallons.

The City of Burlingame Building Division issues plumbing permits for heater replacements. We pull the permit, schedule the inspection and make sure the discharge line and venting pass.

Burlingame waives its permit fee when a licensed contractor replaces a gas water heater or furnace with a heat pump, though state fees still apply.

The 2028 Air District Rule and Your Choice

The Bay Area Air District voted 16 to 1 in 2026 to delay its zero-NOx standard for gas water heaters from January 2027 to January 2028. The standard applies when you buy a new heater and has exemptions for income, small tanks and some retrofit barriers. It does not force you to replace a working heater.

If you are replacing now, a standard gas unit is still available. If you want to avoid the question later, a heat pump unit is worth pricing, and we explain the numbers for your panel and space.
