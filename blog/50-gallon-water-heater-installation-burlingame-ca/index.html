Installing a 50-Gallon Water Heater: A Burlingame Guide

A 50-gallon water heater is the common size for a family of three to five, and installing one correctly means a level base, two earthquake straps, a relief valve and discharge line, proper venting for gas and a permit. Done properly, the work takes most of a day.

This guide covers the parts of a good installation, the California and Burlingame rules and the questions to ask any installer.

By the Burlingame Pro Plumbing team | Updated November 16, 2026

Is 50 Gallons the Right Size

Fifty gallons suits households of three to five people with two bathrooms. A smaller household can often use 40 gallons, and a larger one with a soaking tub and several showers in a row may want more.

Size by peak demand, not headcount. If you run two showers and a dishwasher in the same hour, a 50-gallon tank with a good recovery rate keeps up. A gas tank recovers faster than an electric one, which matters for heavy morning use.

Parts of a Code-Compliant Install

  • Level, stable base, with a pan in finished areas.
  • Two seismic straps, one in the upper third and one in the lower third of the tank.
  • Temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge line to a safe location.
  • Gas shutoff and a flexible gas connector for gas heaters.
  • Proper vent with draft hood and correct slope.
  • Expansion tank on the cold line when the system is closed.
  • Dielectric unions or nipples where copper meets steel.
  • A permit and final inspection.

California and Burlingame Rules You Should Know

Strapping, permits and air rules

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires new and replacement water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped to resist falling or horizontal displacement during an earthquake. It applies to standard residential tanks up to 120 gallons where a preengineered kit is available.

Water heater replacements need a plumbing permit from the Burlingame Building Division. In 2026, the Bay Area Air District delayed its zero-NOx gas water heater standard from January 2027 to January 2028, and the standard applies when you buy a new unit.

If you plan to replace a gas heater with a heat pump, the Burlingame permit fee is waived when a licensed contractor does the work. State fees still apply.

Clearance, Venting and Combustion Air

Gas heaters need space around the burner for combustion air and a vent that carries exhaust outside. A heater in a closet needs louvered openings or ducted air, and one in a garage needs protection from vehicle impact.

Keep combustibles away, and do not store paint, cleaners or gasoline near a gas heater. Vapors can ignite from the pilot or burner. A raised platform in some garages is a code requirement, and we check this on site.

Gas or Electric for a 50-Gallon Tank

A gas 50-gallon tank recovers hot water faster and often costs less to run where gas is available. It needs a gas line, a vent and combustion air. An electric 50-gallon tank has no vent and is simpler to place, but it recovers more slowly and may raise electric bills.

If you plan to move to an all-electric home, a heat pump unit of 50 gallons uses far less electricity than a standard electric tank. It needs room and an outlet, and Burlingame waives the permit fee for a heat pump that replaces a gas water heater or furnace when a licensed contractor does the work.

Think about your future plans. A house that will add solar or an electric vehicle charger may have a case for electric, and a house with a gas range and furnace may be happier with gas.

Common Mistakes in a 50-Gallon Install

  • Skipping the second strap or fastening straps to drywall only.
  • Capping or reducing the relief valve discharge pipe.
  • Forgetting an expansion tank in a closed system.
  • Running the vent with too little pitch or too many bends.
  • Leaving a flex gas connector kinked or rubbing against framing.
  • Installing the heater on a floor that is not level or that can flood without a pan.

Most of these are quick to prevent and costly to fix later. A good installer checks each one and shows you the result. Ask to see the strapping and the relief line before the job is closed out, and keep the permit sign-off.

Questions to Ask an Installer

  • Does the quote include strapping, expansion tank, permit and haul-away?
  • What brand, model and warranty does the heater carry?
  • How will the vent and discharge line be run?
  • Will you test combustion for a gas heater?
  • Who handles the inspection?
