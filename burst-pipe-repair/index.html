First steps

Stop the Water in the First Five Minutes

The main shutoff is the single most useful thing to know in a plumbing emergency. In most Peninsula homes it sits where the water line enters the house, often at the front near the hose bib, in the garage or at a meter box outside. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Close the main valve

A ball valve closes with a quarter turn. An older gate valve takes several full turns, so keep going until it stops.

Open the lowest faucet

Draining the pipes relieves pressure and lets the leak run out instead of spraying.

Kill power near the water

If water reaches outlets or the panel, switch off the breakers from a dry spot or call the utility.

Call and document

Photograph the damage for insurance, then call (650) 860-1670 so a plumber heads your way.

What We Do Once We Arrive

The plumber confirms the water is off, finds the break and checks the pipe on either side of it. A burst section usually means more damage nearby, so we test the neighboring run for weak spots before closing the wall.

The failed section is cut out and replaced with new pipe and fittings that match the material of the line. In older homes, that sometimes means transition fittings between copper and galvanized, or a conversation about replacing the whole run.

After the repair we restore pressure slowly, check every joint for drips and confirm the meter stops moving. You leave with the water on and the repair tested.

The California Plumbing Code is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) published by IAPMO, and it requires replacement pipe and fittings to be materials approved for drinking water, so the new section is never a leftover scrap.

Why Pipes Burst in Burlingame Homes

Freeze bursts are rare on the Peninsula, so most failures here come from pipe age, corrosion, high pressure and water hammer. Galvanized steel and early copper lines in older Burlingame houses corrode from the inside, and a pinhole becomes a rupture under pressure.

Pressure is a common hidden cause. The city runs booster pumps and pressure-reducing valves above 120 feet in elevation, so pressure differs between the flats and the hills. A house that sits at the high end of the range puts extra stress on every joint, and we test it with a gauge as part of a burst pipe visit.

Earthquake movement can also pull a joint or crack a line without any visible damage right away. If the house has been through a shake, a slow leak that appears days later is worth a call to our leak detection team.

Safety

What to Do While You Wait for Us

  • Keep the main valve closed until the repair is finished.
  • Stay clear of any water near outlets, cords or the electrical panel.
  • Do not walk on sagging ceilings, which can hold a large amount of water?
  • Do not use a vacuum on standing water?
  • Move furniture and valuables out of the wet area.
  • Take photographs of the damage before you start cleanup.

If the water has reached the panel or outlets, leave the area and call the power utility or 911 before anything else. Everything else can wait for the plumber.

After the repair

Insurance and Drying Out

Many homeowner policies cover sudden pipe bursts, though not slow leaks that went on for months. Take photographs before anything is moved, keep the damaged pipe section if the plumber gives it to you, and call your insurer promptly.

Drying the building matters as much as the pipe repair. Wet drywall and insulation can grow mold in a couple of days, so schedule a drying company or open the wall cavity quickly.
