Warning signs

Signs Water Is Leaking Under the Foundation

A slab leak is a leak in a pressurized pipe that runs through or beneath the concrete floor. You rarely see it directly. You notice the effects, and by then water may have been working at the soil and subfloor for some time.

Hot spot on the floor

A warm patch on tile or wood can mean a hot water line is leaking underneath.

Meter spins with taps off

If the dial or leak indicator moves while nothing runs, water is escaping from a line.

Water sound in a quiet house

A hiss or trickle at night in a slab home is worth tracing.

Damp carpet or lifting floor

Moisture coming up through a slab can swell wood and loosen tile.

If you suspect a slab leak, shut off the main valve and call right away. Continued flow can undermine the soil and damage flooring.

A Slab Leak That Cannot Wait

Water rising through a floor, a warm spot on tile or a hot water bill that jumps all point to a pressurized line under the slab. Running water with every tap off is another sign. Close the main valve first, then call, because water left running can undermine soil and flooring.

We treat an active slab leak as an urgent call. The emergency line is answered every hour, and a plumber starts with listening gear to pinpoint the leak before any concrete is opened.

How We Confirm a Slab Leak Before Cutting

Guessing is expensive when the floor is concrete. We isolate the plumbing system to confirm the leak is under the slab, then use listening equipment and thermal imaging to place it within a small area.

A line locator helps trace which pipe runs where, so the access point lands on the right run. Our broader leak detection process covers each tool in more detail, and a slab leak repair builds on exactly that work.

Any rerouted or replaced supply line follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and larger piping changes need a city permit and inspection.

Three Ways to Repair a Slab Leak

There is no single fix, since the right approach depends on the pipe, the location and your floors. We present the options with their tradeoffs and let you choose.

Slab leak repair options
MethodBest whenTradeoff
Spot repairOne failed section in good pipe.Requires opening the slab at the leak.
Reroute the linePipe under the slab is failing or hard to reach.New line runs through walls or the attic.
Repipe the affected areaSeveral leaks or old galvanized or copper.Larger project but fixes the cause.

A reroute avoids the concrete entirely, which protects tile and finished floors. A spot repair is faster when there is one clean break.

Safety

What Not to Do With a Suspected Slab Leak

  • Do not break into the floor to look for the pipe yourself?
  • Do not keep running hot water to test whether the floor warms up?
  • Do not ignore a warm floor spot, since a hot line leak can scald or damage flooring?
  • Do not run electrical equipment on a wet floor?
  • Do not wait for a high bill to prove it, because the meter test is faster?

Close the main shutoff if water is visible and call. We locate the leak before any concrete is opened.

Burlingame Homes and Slab Plumbing

Not every Burlingame house sits on a slab. Many older homes have raised foundations with crawlspaces, where leaks are easier to reach, and hillside lots often use different foundation designs. Slab leaks show up in postwar additions, garages converted to living space and some newer builds.

If your house has a crawlspace, a leak in the supply lines is a simpler repair, and our crew will check that before assuming the slab. When the line is the one running from the meter to the house, it falls under water line repair.

Insurance

What to Tell Your Insurance Company

Policies treat slab leaks differently, so read yours or ask your agent. Some cover the cost to access and repair the pipe, some cover only the resulting water damage and some exclude slab leaks.
