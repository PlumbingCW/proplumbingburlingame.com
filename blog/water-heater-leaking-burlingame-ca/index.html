Water Heater Leaking: What the Location Tells You

A water heater that leaks from a fitting, valve or pipe on top is often repairable. A leak from the bottom of the tank or the body means the tank has failed and needs replacement. In either case, close the cold supply valve above the heater, turn off the power or gas and call a plumber.

The location of the leak tells you most of what you need to know.

First Steps in the First Five Minutes

Shut off the cold supply

Turn the valve above the heater clockwise to stop water feeding the leak.

Turn off the energy

Switch off the breaker for electric heaters, or turn the gas control to pilot or off.

Protect the area

Move stored items and lay towels. If the leak is large, drain the tank with a hose.

Why Tanks Fail

A steel tank is lined with glass, and an anode rod inside sacrifices itself to protect the steel. When the rod is used up and the lining cracks, the tank corrodes through. Burlingame water is very soft, with an average hardness near 32 mg/L in the 2022 city report, so scale is not the usual cause. Age and pressure are.

High pressure adds stress to the tank and fittings. The city uses pressure-reducing valves and booster pumps above 120 feet in elevation, and a house on the high end of the pressure range may see more wear. We measure it with a gauge on the hose bib.

Tank Leaks Cannot Be Repaired

A leak through the tank itself cannot be patched. Epoxy and sealants do not last on a pressurized steel tank, and the water will keep coming. If the tank is leaking, replacement is the safe path.

Water heater replacement involves a permit and earthquake strapping under California Health and Safety Code section 19211, and we handle both on our water heater replacement visits. For fittings and valves, a water heater repair visit is enough.

Water Damage to Watch For

Water from a heater can soak the floor, the wall behind and the ceiling below. Check for damp drywall, a musty smell and swelling in baseboards. If the leak ran for a while, look for hidden damage and consider a leak detection check if you cannot tell where the water went.

Dry the area quickly. Mold can start in a day or two on wet drywall.

Cleaning Up and Drying Out

If the heater leaked onto a garage slab, mop and dry the floor and run a fan. If it leaked onto a finished floor, check the baseboard and the wall behind it. Water wicks up drywall and can reach insulation. Pull back baseboard if needed and look at the wall cavity.

Moisture meters let a contractor measure how wet materials are, and a dry-out company can place drying equipment. Do not rely on touch alone. Drywall can feel dry on the surface and still be wet inside, and mold begins within a couple of days.

Keep the damaged parts, such as a corroded fitting, and take photos with a date. If you claim the loss, the insurer will want evidence of when and how it happened.

Repair, Replace or Upgrade

When a fitting or valve is the problem, a repair is quick and inexpensive. When the tank is leaking, replacement is the only option, and that is a good moment to think about what you want next. A standard tank, a heat pump or a tankless unit each have their place.

The Bay Area Air District delayed its zero-NOx gas water heater standard to January 2028 in 2026. A standard gas tank is still available today, and a heat pump meets the standard. In Burlingame, a heat pump replacing a gas water heater by a licensed contractor also has its permit fee waived. Our tankless water heater page covers on-demand options.

Whatever you choose, ask for strapping, an expansion tank where needed and a drain pan in a finished space. A pan with a drain line protects the floor if the next tank fails years from now.

Questions to Ask the Plumber

Ask where exactly the leak is, whether the tank can be saved and what the repair or replacement includes. Ask about strapping, an expansion tank, a drain pan and the permit. Ask how long the work will take and when hot water returns.

A good plumber answers these in plain terms and puts the price in writing before starting. If an answer is vague, ask again.

Prevention

How to Prevent a Heater Leak

  • Test the relief valve once a year.
  • Have the anode rod checked as the heater ages.
  • Flush sediment from the tank each year.
  • Install a drain pan with a drain line in finished areas.
  • Keep water pressure within a normal range.
  • Replace the heater before it is far past its expected life.

A heater that has been dripping from the bottom for weeks is overdue. Call us for a written price.
