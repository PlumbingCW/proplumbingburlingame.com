Garbage Disposal Installation: Steps and What to Expect

Installing a garbage disposal takes a few hours and involves electrical, drain and mounting steps. If you are replacing a unit and the electrical is already in place, a handy homeowner can do it. A first-time install needs an outlet and a switch, and that is often where a plumber and an electrician come in.

Below is the process, the choices that matter and the mistakes that cause leaks.

Choosing the Right Disposal

Disposals are sold by horsepower, usually from one third to one horsepower. A larger motor grinds faster and jams less, and a continuous-feed model is the common type for homes. Batch-feed models run only with the lid in place and are a safer option when children are around.

Check that the unit fits your sink flange size, and that the discharge outlet lines up with your drain. Sound insulation varies, and a more expensive unit is usually quieter.

Rough guide to sizing
HouseholdTypical motor
One or two people, light cookingOne third to one half horsepower.
Family with regular cookingThree quarters horsepower.
Heavy use or a large familyOne horsepower.

Power and Switch Requirements

A disposal needs a switched outlet or a hardwired connection under the sink. If you already have one, the replacement is straightforward. If you do not, a new outlet and wall switch must be installed by a qualified person, and that may need a permit.

Turn off the breaker before touching any wiring and verify the circuit is dead with a tester. Never rely on the wall switch alone.

The Installation Steps

Remove the old unit

Disconnect the drain, the dishwasher hose and the power, then twist the unit off the mounting ring.

Set the sink flange

Clean the sink opening, apply plumber's putty around the flange and press it in. Fit the mounting ring and tighten.

Prepare the unit

Knock out the dishwasher plug if there is a dishwasher, and connect the wiring in the access plate.

Mount and connect

Hang the unit on the mounting ring, align the discharge, and connect the drain trap and dishwasher hose.

Run water and check every joint for drips. Then run the unit with water to confirm it is quiet and drains well.

The Dishwasher Knockout Mistake

Many disposals have a plug inside the dishwasher inlet that must be knocked out with a hammer and screwdriver before connecting the dishwasher hose. If it is left in place, the dishwasher cannot drain, and the water backs up into the dishwasher.

Remove the plug and fish out the piece that falls into the unit. It is a common error and it takes a minute to avoid.

Drain Connections That Hold

The trap and drain connections are where leaks start. Use new gaskets and washers and tighten slip nuts by hand plus a quarter turn. Over-tightening plastic nuts can crack them.

If the drain is slow before you install, fix that first. A clogged line will make a new disposal back up. A drain cleaning visit solves it, and our garbage disposal page covers repair and replacement.

Tools and Parts You Will Need

  • Screwdrivers, a hammer and a hex wrench for the jam-clearing socket.
  • Plumber's putty or the gasket supplied with the unit.
  • A new trap and drain parts if the old ones are corroded.
  • Wire connectors, a cable clamp and a power cord if the disposal does not include one.
  • A bucket and towels for water in the trap.
  • A voltage tester to confirm power is off.

Check that the new disposal comes with a mounting assembly and whether it includes a power cord. Some units sell the cord separately, and installing one is a simple job that needs the circuit to be off.

Measure the distance from the sink drain to the wall drain before you buy, and check the clearance under the sink so the unit does not hit the trap or a shelf.

Measure the distance from the sink drain to the wall drain before you buy. A disposal is taller than a simple trap, and the discharge may not line up with the existing plumbing. A short extension or a new trap arm fixes it.

Care After Installation

A disposal lasts longer when it is used correctly. Run cold water while it grinds and for about fifteen seconds after, so the waste is flushed down the drain. Cold water keeps grease solid so it breaks apart and travels, where hot water melts it and lets it coat the pipe.

Avoid fibrous items such as celery, corn husks and artichokes, and keep grease, coffee grounds in large amounts, eggshells in large amounts and bones out. A disposal cannot grind everything, and the pipe past it is the real limit. In a house with older drain lines, such as many of Burlingame's older homes, a clogged kitchen line is common and the disposal gets blamed.

If the kitchen drain still slows after installation, a drain cleaning visit clears the line, and the plumber can show you what is on the pipe walls.

Code Points Behind a Clean Install

The trap and drain connections under a sink follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The dishwasher drain also needs backflow protection, usually an air gap or a high loop, so dirty water cannot siphon back into the dishwasher. A good installer checks both before closing the cabinet.
