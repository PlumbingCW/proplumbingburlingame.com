The neighborhood

About Burlingame Park

Burlingame Park is one of the oldest named residential areas in the city. The Burlingame Historical Society begins its neighborhood tour here, and real estate guides place it west of El Camino Real, bordered on one side by Hillsborough, near Pershing Park and downtown Burlingame Avenue.

Because the boundaries are not formally mapped in any source we found, we describe the area by those anchors. If you are unsure whether your street counts as Burlingame Park, call and we can still tell you whether we serve the address.

Older Homes, Large Trees and Your Lines

Burlingame calls itself the City of Trees and counts more than 18,000 public trees, and a 1908 ordinance protects them. Mature trees are part of the look of Burlingame Park, and roots are a well known reason that older sewer laterals slow down.

That does not mean every home here has a root problem. It does mean a camera look is the sensible first step when a drain keeps backing up. A sewer camera inspection shows the pipe, its joints and any roots before anyone digs.

Homes with a long run from the street to the house have more pipe to inspect. If a stoppage returns after a cable clearing, ask about hydro jetting to clean the pipe wall, and about sewer line repair if the camera finds damage.

Water Heaters in Larger Homes

Bigger houses often have long hot water runs, and a tank in a garage or closet can be a long way from the bathroom that complains first. Waiting for hot water, or running out during a full house, is a reason to review the heater size and the pipe route.

When a tank fails, replacement needs a plumbing permit from the Burlingame Building Division, and California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires the new heater to be braced or strapped. We cover both on a water heater replacement visit, and a smaller fix can be a water heater repair.

What Burlingame Water Means Here

A citywide fact that applies to Burlingame Park

Burlingame buys all of its water from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission, and the 2022 water quality report lists average hardness at 32 mg/L, which is very soft.

Very soft water leaves little scale, so a failing fixture here is more often age or wear than mineral buildup. Repairs follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). A leak detection visit is the better next step when a meter moves with every tap off.
