Water Meter Running When Nothing Is On

If the water meter dial or the red leak indicator moves when every tap and appliance is off, water is escaping from your system. The usual culprits are a running toilet, a leaking irrigation line, a failed water heater fitting or a broken pipe in the yard.

This guide shows how to read the meter and narrow the leak down before you call a plumber.

By the Burlingame Pro Plumbing team | Updated November 16, 2026

How to Read the Meter

The meter sits in a box near the street or property line. Lift the cover carefully, since insects and debris can hide there. Wipe the glass and look for a row of digits, which is the total, and a small dial or triangle, which is the leak indicator.

The leak indicator spins when even a small amount of water passes. If it is turning, water is moving. Some meters show cubic feet and others gallons, and a note on the face tells you the unit and the value of each digit.

The Step-by-Step Test

Turn everything off

Close taps, stop the washer and dishwasher and pause irrigation and ice makers.

Check the indicator

Watch for movement. If it moves, note how fast.

Write down the reading

Record the digits and wait 15 minutes without using water.

Compare

Read again. Any change confirms a leak.

Then close the main shutoff to the house. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the shutoff.

What the Bill Shows in Burlingame

The City of Burlingame reads your meter and bills for water, sewer and other services. A sudden jump over a similar period is a clue, and the bill may show usage by day. A steady flow at all hours, including overnight, supports a leak.

If the meter test shows no leak and the bill still looks wrong, ask the city Water Division to review your reading. Weather, irrigation, guests and seasonal use can all explain a higher bill.

When the Leak Is Underground or Hidden

If the meter moves with the main closed or you cannot find the source inside the house, the leak may be in a wall, under a slab or in the yard. Locating it takes equipment, and guessing can lead to digging in the wrong place.

Our leak detection service uses listening devices and pressure tests to pinpoint the leak. A buried service line leak is repaired through water line repair.

Common Hidden Leaks That Move the Meter

A running toilet is the most frequent cause of a spinning meter in a house with nothing else wrong. The flapper warps with age, or the fill valve sticks, and water trickles from the tank into the bowl all day. It makes little sound and shows up only on the bill.

Irrigation leaks come second. A cracked valve, a broken sprinkler head or a buried line with a loose fitting can run for weeks without anything showing on the surface. Drip lines can also be chewed or pulled apart. Test by closing the irrigation shutoff and watching the meter.

Water heaters and softeners can leak too, and a hose bib with a bad vacuum breaker can drip into a bed where it goes unnoticed. Walk the property after a rainless day and look for any spot that is wet or unusually green.

Checking Daily Use and Seasonal Patterns

If your bill shows daily use, compare weekdays and weekends and check overnight values. A house with no leak shows near-zero use between midnight and morning. A leak shows as a steady baseline at all hours, even when everyone is asleep.

Seasonal changes matter too. Irrigation, a guest in the house or a new fixture adds to the total. Compare the same period last year and look for a gradual climb. A leak tends to appear as a sudden step up that stays high.

Keep a short log for a week. Write the meter reading each morning and each evening and note what water you used. The log will show the leak rate and tell the plumber how big the problem is.

Fixing the Common Causes Yourself

A toilet flapper is a part you can replace in ten minutes, and it fixes the most common silent leak. Turn off the supply, flush to empty the tank, unhook the old flapper and clip in a matching one. Check the chain length so it has a little slack and does not hold the flapper open.

Dripping faucets usually need a new cartridge or washer, and a hose bib needs a new vacuum breaker or washer. If a fix does not stop the meter, the leak is somewhere else. A plumber can take it from there.

Repairs That Meet Code

Once a leak is found, replacement piping and fittings must be approved for drinking water under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). A repair that uses the wrong material can fail early and may not pass a permit inspection, so use a licensed plumber for anything beyond a flapper or a washer.
