The neighborhood

About Burlingame Terrace

Burlingame Terrace is a residential subdivision bounded by Oak Grove Avenue, Broadway, El Camino Real and California Drive. It was surveyed in 1905, grew quickly after the 1906 San Francisco earthquake, and includes a Tudor Revival village near Willborough Road and Neuchatel Avenue.

Real estate guides describe homes built in the early twentieth century with woodwork, stone facades and wide porches, along with a mix of styles and some newer homes. Each house is different, so the right advice starts with a look.

Homes of This Era and Their Cleanouts

Burlingame did not require a cleanout in buildings before the mid-1960s. If your Terrace home was built before then, it may have no cleanout at the right-of-way, which makes clearing a lateral slower and often leads to installing one during a repair.

We cannot say what pipes are in a particular house without seeing them. Camera inspection through sewer inspection is how we tell clay, cast iron or plastic apart, and a sewer line cleaning visit can clear the stoppage while you decide on a repair.

Water Heater Choices and the Heat Pump Rule

Many older homes have a tank in a garage, basement or closet with limited space. When it is time to replace, the choice includes a standard tank, a tankless unit or a heat pump model.

What the city and the air district say

The Bay Area Air Quality Management District voted 16 to 1 in 2026 to delay its zero-NOx standard for water heaters from January 2027 to January 2028. It applies at purchase, with exemptions.

Burlingame waives the permit fee when a heat pump replaces a gas water heater or furnace, which can matter for a Terrace home that needs a replacement.

Walkable Streets and Busy Parking

Terrace is close to Burlingame High School, Broadway and Burlingame Avenue, so street parking can be tight. Tell us where to park when you call, and clear the driveway and the path to the water heater so a visit goes smoothly.
