Sewage Backups Are Never a Wait-and-See Problem

Raw sewage carries bacteria and can ruin flooring and drywall in hours. If wastewater is backing up, stop running water, keep everyone away from the affected area and call. Do not use chemical openers, which add hazards for the plumber who has to open the line.

Sewage at floor drains or tubs

Wastewater rising at the lowest fixture means the main line cannot carry the flow.

Repeated clogs after cabling

A line that closes up again within weeks usually has a break, a belly or roots, not just a clog.

Gurgling plus sewer odor

Air pushed back through traps signals a restriction in the lateral.

Sinkhole or soggy yard patch

Wet ground or a sunken spot along the sewer route can mean a collapsed pipe.

What Burlingame Requires of Your Sewer Lateral

City rules that shape a repair

The City of Burlingame maintains the public sewer from the right-of-way cleanout out to the main. The pipe from there to your house is your lateral. Buildings from before the mid-1960s were not required to have a right-of-way cleanout, so older homes may need one added as part of the repair.

A lateral test is required when a property with buildings older than 25 years is sold or transferred, and repairs have to be completed and approved before title moves. An encroachment permit comes from Public Works and plumbing permits for repairs, backwater valves and cleanouts come from the Building Division.

The test checks the lateral for water loss, so a line that holds water can pass even if roots or a bad grade are present. That is why a camera look beforehand is worth the time, and why we explain the difference between a passing test and a healthy pipe.

The city allows one free retest, and the certificate it issues lasts ten years, or twenty-five years after a full replacement. Planning repairs around that timeline saves a seller from rushing a dig the week before closing.

Replacement lateral pipe, cleanouts and backwater valves follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and each needs a plumbing permit.

Diagnosing the Break With a Camera

A camera inspection shows whether the line is cracked, offset, root-filled or collapsed, and how far from the cleanout the problem sits. That is the difference between a small spot repair and a larger replacement.

We record the footage so you can see it too. If you need it for a sale or an insurance claim, our sewer camera inspection page explains what the report contains.

Aftercare

Protecting the Repair Afterward

A repaired lateral should come with two upgrades worth considering. A cleanout at the right-of-way gives the next plumber or the city a way in, and a backwater valve stops sewage from the public main flowing back into a house with low fixtures. Both need a plumbing permit from the Building Division, and we handle that paperwork.

Roots will look for the next joint if the surrounding clay is old. Ask us about periodic camera checks or scheduled jetting if the lateral runs under a mature tree, and keep grease and wipes out of the line.
