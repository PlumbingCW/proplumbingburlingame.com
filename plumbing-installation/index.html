Remodel Plumbing in Burlingame

What the city expects

Burlingame's Indoor Water Conservation Ordinance applies to permitted new construction and to kitchen and bath remodels. It sets toilets at 1.28 gallons per flush or less, showers at 2.0 gallons per minute, bathroom faucets at 1.5 and kitchen faucets at 2.2.

The Building Division issues the plumbing permit for the work, and its counter is open in the morning on weekdays. We prepare the permit, schedule inspections and make sure fixtures meet the limits before they go in.

New fixtures and piping must follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the Building Division inspects both the rough-in and the final.

Old Houses and New Plumbing

Adding a bathroom to an older Burlingame house often means tying into old drains and supply lines. Cast iron and clay lines may be fine, or they may be the reason to upgrade while the wall is open. We check before connecting so a new fixture does not stress an old line.

Water pressure matters too. If the house sits on the high end of the city's pressure range, we add or adjust a regulator. Where an old supply system is failing, repiping at the same time saves opening walls twice.

Kitchens, Bathrooms and Laundry Rooms

Kitchen remodels move the sink, add a pot filler or an island sink, and add a dishwasher and disposal. Each one needs a supply line, a drain with proper slope and a vent path, and an island sink is the hard one because the vent has to loop under the floor. We plan that early so the cabinet layout still works.

Bathrooms are about fixture clearances and water use. A shower valve needs to sit where the wall framing allows, a toilet needs its flange at the right distance from the wall, and a double vanity needs two drains tied into one vent. Laundry rooms need a standpipe, a trap and a shutoff, and a washer box that does not squeal when the machine fills.

We walk the space with you before anything is cut, and we mark the pipe routes so the cabinet maker and tile setter know what to expect.

ADUs, Additions and Garage Conversions

A backyard unit or a garage conversion needs its own sewer connection and water supply. The sewer tie-in has to meet the existing lateral at a point that has the right depth and capacity, and the city requires permits and inspections for every step.

We camera the existing lateral before designing the connection, because an old clay line may need replacing before it carries more fixtures. If the existing line needs work, see sewer line repair. The water supply may also need a larger line or a second shutoff, and we size it to the added fixtures.

Gas for a new kitchen or water heater is planned at the same time, so the permit covers the whole scope.
