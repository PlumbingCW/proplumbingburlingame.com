Tub and Shower Faucets Are Different

A leaking tub spout or a showerhead that drips after shutoff points to the valve behind the wall. Repair usually means removing the trim and pulling the cartridge, which is quick when parts are available and the wall is accessible.

If the valve body itself is corroded, the repair means opening the wall. We tell you the likelihood before we start, and we avoid cutting until the cause is confirmed. A leak inside the wall behind a shower is worth a leak detection check before you reseal anything.

Water Pressure and the Burlingame Hills

A faucet that sprays, spits or hammers may be telling you the pressure is high. The city uses pressure-reducing valves and booster pumps in areas above 120 feet in elevation, and some homes see more pressure than the fixtures were built for.

A pressure gauge on the hose bib tells us in a minute. If the pressure is high, a regulator protects every faucet, toilet fill valve and appliance supply in the house. It is a small step that avoids repeated repairs.

When we choose a replacement fixture, the flow rate has to respect Burlingame's water conservation ordinance and the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Kitchen Faucets and the Sink Beneath Them

Kitchen faucets work harder than any other fixture in the house, and the cabinet below shows it. Corroded supply lines, a leaking angle stop and a loose drain connection are all common while the faucet itself is working fine.

When we repair a kitchen faucet, we check the whole sink base, including the garbage disposal connection, the trap and the dishwasher line. Finding a slow drip early protects the cabinet floor, which swells and rots when it stays wet. If the disposal is the leaking part, our garbage disposal service covers it.
