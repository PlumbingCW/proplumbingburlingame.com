A toilet that clogs repeatedly may point beyond the toilet itself. If every fixture is slow, the problem is in the line and drain cleaning is the right call.

When the Base Leaks

A leak around the bottom of a toilet often means the wax ring has failed. The ring sits between the bowl and the drain flange, and when it fails, water seeps onto the subfloor and rots it slowly. A faint smell of sewer near the toilet is a clue.

Replacing the ring means lifting the toilet, inspecting the flange and setting the bowl on fresh seal. If the flange is cracked or the floor has softened, those get fixed too, since the new seal will not hold on a damaged base.

Do not caulk around the base as a fix. It traps water and hides the leak until the floor fails.

Repair or Replace the Toilet

A toilet with a failed seal, flapper or fill valve is usually worth repairing. A toilet with a cracked bowl or tank, repeated clogs due to design or old high-flow parts is a better candidate for replacement.

Burlingame's indoor water conservation ordinance sets toilets at 1.28 gallons per flush or less for permitted new construction and kitchen or bath remodels. A replacement toilet that meets that rating also saves water in an older home, and we can install one the same visit. If a bathroom is being rebuilt, our plumbing installation team handles the rest of the rough-in.

Replacement toilets also have to meet the flush volume limits in the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Plunger First, Plumber Second

A flange plunger is worth a try on a simple clog, using steady pushes rather than quick jabs. If the water drains slowly and then clears, you are done. If it stays high or returns within a day, the blockage is past the trap and a closet auger or camera is the next tool.

Toys, wipes and excess paper cause most toilet clogs, and a toilet that clogs again and again may have a partly blocked drain behind it. If the tub or sink gurgles when you flush, treat it as a main line problem and call us.

We use a closet auger that clears the bowl without scratching the porcelain, and we test with several flushes before we leave.
