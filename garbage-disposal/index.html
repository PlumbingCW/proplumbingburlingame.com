Disposal Problems That Cannot Wait

A jammed disposal is a nuisance. A disposal that leaks into the cabinet is damage in progress, and a kitchen sink that will not drain at all means the problem is beyond the disposal. Turn off the unit at the wall switch and, if the cabinet is wet, close the angle stops under the sink.

Humming but not spinning

The blades are jammed. Never put your hand in the unit.

Water leaking from the bottom or sides

A cracked housing or failed seal. This unit usually needs replacement.

Sink full of standing water

The drain line past the disposal is blocked, not just the grinder.

Burning smell or a tripped breaker

The motor is overheating or has failed and the unit should stay off.

A leaking disposal ruins a cabinet

Call now and a plumber heads out.

What We Do When We Replace a Disposal

Remove the old unit

We disconnect the electrical, drain and dishwasher lines and release the mounting ring.

Reset the flange

A new seal or plumber's putty goes in, so the sink flange does not leak.

Mount and connect

The new unit is mounted, the drain and dishwasher hose are connected and the knockout plug is cleared if needed.

Test the whole sink

We run water, check every joint and confirm the drain flows freely.

We size the unit to the household. A larger motor handles more, and a standard unit suits most kitchens.

Protecting the Kitchen Drain Beyond the Disposal

Most disposals that fail early are asked to handle things they were not built for. Grease, coffee grounds in volume, fibrous peels and pasta swell or coat the pipe past the unit and cause the real clog.

If the sink backs up even with a working disposal, the blockage is in the line. A drain cleaning visit clears it and shows whether grease is building up. Restaurants and busy households with heavy use may benefit from scheduled jetting, and our hydro jetting page explains how.
