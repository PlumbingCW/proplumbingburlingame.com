Reverse Osmosis Under the Sink: Worth It in Burlingame?

An under-sink reverse osmosis system removes most dissolved solids and many tastes from your drinking water, and it is worth it if you dislike the taste of chloramine or want very low mineral water. For many Burlingame households, a carbon filter does the taste job for less money and less waste.

Here is what the system does, what Burlingame water contains and how installation and upkeep work.

By the Burlingame Pro Plumbing team | Updated November 16, 2026

What Burlingame Tap Water Already Has

Burlingame buys all of its water from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission, mostly Hetch Hetchy snowmelt. The city's 2022 water quality report lists total dissolved solids from less than 20 to 104 ppm with an average of 61, and hardness averaging 32 mg/L. That is low for any municipal supply.

The supply is disinfected with chloramine and treated with UV. The chloramine is what gives some people a pool-like taste or smell. Because the minerals are already low, RO removes less than it would in a hard-water town.

How an RO System Works

Water passes through a sediment filter, a carbon filter and then a semipermeable membrane that rejects dissolved solids. The clean water collects in a small pressurized tank and flows to a dedicated faucet. Rejected water, with the concentrated solids, goes down the drain.

A final carbon stage polishes the taste. Some systems add a remineralizing cartridge that puts back a small amount of calcium and magnesium so the water does not taste flat.

  • Sediment prefilter catches particles.
  • Carbon prefilter reduces chlorine and chloramine taste.
  • Membrane removes dissolved solids.
  • Storage tank holds treated water.
  • Post filter polishes taste.
  • Dedicated faucet delivers the water.

RO or a Simple Carbon Filter

Carbon filter

  • Reduces chloramine taste and odor.
  • Costs less to buy and run.
  • No water sent to drain.
  • Small footprint under the sink.

Reverse osmosis

  • Removes most dissolved solids as well.
  • Needs a drain connection and more space.
  • Sends some water to the drain.
  • Higher upfront and filter cost.

If taste is your only complaint, start with a carbon filter rated for chloramine. If you want very low mineral content for a coffee machine or ice maker, RO makes more sense.

Space and Plumbing You Need

The unit takes room under the sink, usually a cabinet bay about the size of a small trash can plus the tank. A cabinet with a disposal, a dishwasher line and cleaning supplies can feel crowded, so we measure first.

The install adds a feed valve on the cold line, a drain saddle on the sink drain with an air gap or high loop and a faucet hole in the sink or counter. If your sink has no spare hole, a new faucet or a drilled hole is needed. Our water filtration page covers the options.

Installing and Maintaining an RO System

Mount the unit

The filter housings and tank mount on the cabinet wall with room to change cartridges.

Tap the supply

A feed valve ties into the cold line below the sink.

Connect the drain

A saddle clamps onto the drain pipe above the trap with an air gap faucet or a high loop.

Flush and test

We flush the system, check for leaks and let the tank fill before first use.

Plan to change prefilters about once a year and the membrane every few years, depending on the model. Mark your calendar, since an old filter stops working without any visible sign.

What About a Water Softener

You do not need one in Burlingame. Hardness averages 32 mg/L, which is well under two grains per gallon. A softener adds salt and maintenance for almost no benefit, and our water filtration and softener page explains when one is justified.

If you are installing RO as part of a kitchen remodel, our plumbing installation team can plan the faucet hole and drain tie-in with your contractor.

What Reverse Osmosis Does Not Do

An RO system does not add anything healthy to your water, and it does not clean the water in the rest of the house. It treats only the kitchen tap or whatever it is plumbed to. It is also not a substitute for a medical device when a doctor has given instructions for special water treatment.

RO water is low in minerals, which is why some people find it tastes flat. Remineralizing cartridges and blending valves can improve it. Because Burlingame's source is already soft, the difference between tap and RO is smaller here than in towns with hard water, so many households find a good carbon filter satisfies them.

Any filter system needs maintenance. An old cartridge can grow bacteria and give worse water than none, so keep a schedule and note installation dates on the housing.

Costs to Weigh

  • Equipment and installation, which varies with the system.
  • Replacement filters and membranes over the years.
  • Water sent to the drain with each gallon made.
  • Space in the cabinet and any changes to the sink.
  • Your time to change cartridges or the cost of a service plan.

A fair comparison is the cost per gallon of RO water against bottled water. Most households that drink a lot of filtered water find RO cheaper than bottles, while those who only want a better taste may find a pitcher or a carbon filter enough. We give you an honest recommendation on the phone.

Questions to Ask Before You Buy

  • Does the system need a pump for the water pressure at my house?
  • Will it fit with my disposal and dishwasher?
