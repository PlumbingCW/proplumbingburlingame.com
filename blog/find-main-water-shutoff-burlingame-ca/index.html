How to Find Your Main Water Shutoff Valve

Your main water shutoff valve is on the pipe where the water line enters the house, usually at the front near a hose bib, in the garage, in a utility closet or at the meter. Find it now, label it and make sure everyone in the house knows how to close it.

When a pipe bursts, the difference between a wet floor and a flooded house is how fast someone can reach this valve.

By the Burlingame Pro Plumbing team | Updated November 16, 2026

Where to Look First

Start where the supply enters the building. In many Peninsula houses, the line comes up through the foundation or the wall on the street side. Look near the front hose bib, in the garage along the wall facing the street and in a closet or utility room that backs onto the street.

Older houses may have the valve inside the crawlspace or in a basement-like utility area. Newer homes often have a shutoff in a small box in a planting bed or at the side of the garage.

Ball Valves, Gate Valves and Meter Valves

A ball valve has a lever handle. A quarter turn closes it, and the lever sits across the pipe when it is closed. A gate valve has a round wheel handle that takes several full turns clockwise. A meter valve at the street usually needs a meter key or a wrench.

Gate valves are common in older homes. They can seize with age and they sometimes break if forced. If your valve is a gate valve, test it gently once a year so it stays free.

Burlingame Meter and the City's Side

Burlingame buys its water from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission, and the meter marks where the city's responsibility ends. The valve on the street side of the meter belongs to the city, and its Water Division asks that residents not operate it.

Use the shutoff on your side, after the meter. If the break is before the meter, or if your house valve fails, call the city Water Division during business hours, or the city's after-hours dispatch for an urgent main break.

If the Valve Is Stuck or Leaking

A valve that will not turn, drips from the handle or breaks off needs replacement. Do not use a long pipe on the handle, which can snap the stem. Spray the stem with penetrating oil and work it back and forth gently.

If it will not close, use the meter shutoff as a backup and call a plumber. We replace main valves with a quarter-turn ball valve, which is easier to use and lasts longer. A failed valve can be tied to a larger issue, such as water line repair or a burst pipe repair.

Other Shutoffs Worth Knowing

The main valve is not the only one that matters. Every fixture should have its own shutoff, and knowing where they are lets you stop a small leak without turning off the whole house. Toilets have a valve on the wall or floor behind the bowl, sinks have angle stops under the cabinet and the water heater has a cold supply valve on top.

Washing machines have two valves, one for hot and one for cold, behind the washer. A burst washer hose is a common cause of flooding, so closing those valves when you leave on a trip is a good habit. Dishwashers and refrigerator ice makers have small stops under the sink or behind the unit.

Test these valves once a year by turning them closed and open. Old stops seize or leak when first moved, so it is better to find out on a calm day. If one fails, our faucet repair team can replace it.

What to Do If You Rent or Share a Building

Renters and condo owners may have a shutoff inside the unit or only at a building level. Ask your landlord or property manager where it is and who has the key. In a multi-unit building, a main valve may be in a utility room, and you may not be allowed to use it.

If a pipe bursts and you cannot reach a valve, call the property manager at once and then call a plumber. Do not wait for a callback while water spreads. Tell the dispatcher that the valve is not accessible, and we will advise how to limit the damage until it is closed.

Write the location of every valve on a card and tape it inside a cabinet door. A little preparation prevents panic.

Why Every House Has One

The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), expects a full-open shutoff valve on the building water supply so the house can be isolated. A valve that has not moved in years can seize, which is why we suggest exercising it once a year and replacing a failing gate valve with a quarter-turn ball valve.

Be ready

Make a Plan Before You Need One

  • Find the main valve and label it with a tag.
  • Check that it turns once a year.
  • Show everyone in the house, including teenagers and guests.
  • Know where individual shutoffs are for the toilet, sink and water heater.
  • Keep a wrench or meter key nearby if you need one.

If you smell gas or see electrical hazards near water, leave and call 911 first. Our leak detection team can help if you suspect a leak that is not obvious.
