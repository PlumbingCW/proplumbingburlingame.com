Burlingame Sewer Lateral Test When You Sell

The City of Burlingame requires a sewer lateral test when a property with buildings more than 25 years old is sold or transferred. The lateral is tested in the presence of a city representative, and any defects must be repaired and approved before title can transfer.

This guide is based on the city's published requirements, and the details below are worth confirming with Public Works before you list.

By the Burlingame Pro Plumbing team | Updated November 16, 2026

What the Test Is and Who It Applies To

Your sewer lateral is the pipe from your house to the public sewer. The City of Burlingame maintains the sewer from the right-of-way cleanout out to the main, and the owner is responsible for the lateral on the property side. The sale-time test is part of the city's sewer code in Chapter 15.12.

The requirement applies when the property has buildings older than 25 years and is sold or transferred. It exists to keep groundwater from leaking into the sewer and to find broken laterals before a new owner inherits the problem.

Why a Camera Look First Is Smart

A lateral can pass a water test and still have roots, a belly or a cracked joint that will cause trouble. A lateral can also fail a water test because of a single bad joint that a camera would have found first.

Running a camera before the official test shows what is in the pipe and where. If it shows a break, you can repair it before the city's test and avoid a failed result. Our sewer camera inspection service records the findings and explains the options.

If the Lateral Fails

A failed test means the repair has to be completed and approved before title transfers. The city allows one free retest, and the work needs permits. An Encroachment Permit and the test fee come from Public Works, and plumbing permits for repairs, replacement, backwater valves and cleanouts come from the Building Division.

Older homes may need a cleanout installed at the right-of-way. Buildings from before the mid-1960s were not required to have one, so the repair often includes adding it. See sewer line repair for how repairs are done.

How Long the Certificate Lasts

When the lateral passes, the city issues a certificate valid for 10 years. If the lateral was fully replaced, the certificate lasts 25 years. This is useful for a seller, since the certificate follows the property and a future sale within that period may not need a new test.

Ask Public Works to confirm the current rules and fees. Ordinances change, and staff can tell you what applies to your address.

Who to call

When to Call a Plumber or the City

  • Call Public Works to confirm test rules, fees and permits for your address.
  • Call a plumber when the camera shows roots, cracks, offsets or a belly.
  • Call the city if neighbors are backing up too, which suggests the public main.
  • Call your agent or escrow officer when a failed test could change the closing date.

A plumber and the city handle different parts of the process, and calling the right one first saves time.

What the City Test Does Not Check

The test checks whether the lateral holds water or air. It does not check alignment, grade, pipe material or roots. A lateral that is sagging in one section but not leaking will pass, and it can still back up after you move in. A lateral with a hairline root intrusion above the water line can also pass.

For a buyer, this is a reason to order a camera inspection during the due-diligence period. For a seller, it shows why the test result is not the same as a clean bill of health. Both parties are better informed when they know the condition of the pipe as well as the pass or fail.

The certificate also covers only the lateral, and does not speak to the main in the street, which the city maintains.

Repair Options if the Lateral Needs Work

A lateral that fails can be repaired in several ways, depending on what the camera shows. A short break may need a spot repair. A long run of cracked clay may be best replaced. Trenchless methods avoid digging up a driveway or landscaping, though they do not suit every pipe.

The city requires permits for the work, and a repair must be approved before title transfers. That is why starting early matters. Work may involve the Building Division for plumbing permits and Public Works for the encroachment permit and test fee.

If you are replacing the whole lateral, you receive a certificate valid for 25 years. That is a selling point for the next owner and can make the investment easier to justify.

Who to Contact at the City

Public Works Engineering handles the lateral test and the encroachment permit, and the Building Division handles plumbing permits for repairs, replacement, backwater valves and cleanouts. The Building Division counter is open weekday mornings, and it opens later on Thursdays, so plan your visit.

Bring the property address, a description of the work and the contractor's license details. Permits for lateral work are separate from the test itself, and starting with a phone call can save a wasted trip.

The Sale Test Rules

What Chapter 15.12 asks of a seller

Under Chapter 15.12 of the city code, a building older than twenty-five years needs a lateral test at sale. The water test holds water two feet above the lateral for thirty minutes with a loss under four gallons, and hillside areas use an air test instead. A video inspection is not accepted.

The seller gets one free retest, and the certificate is valid for ten years, or twenty-five years after a full replacement. Public Works handles the encroachment permit and test fee, and the Building Division issues plumbing permits.

Planning

Planning Around a Closing Date

  • Order a camera inspection before listing, not after you are in escrow.
  • Clear the lateral first if roots or grease are present.
  • Schedule the city test early, since repairs take time.
  • Ask your agent how repairs are handled in the contract.
  • Keep the paperwork, since the certificate can help future sales.
