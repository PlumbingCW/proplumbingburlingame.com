Warning signs

When the Line to Your House Fails

The water line is the buried pipe that brings water from the meter to your home. It is your responsibility on the house side of the meter, and it can fail without warning. Low pressure at every fixture, a wet spot over the pipe route or a spike in your bill are the usual clues.

Pressure drops everywhere

If every tap is weak, not just one, suspect the main line or the regulator.

Wet ground over the route

A damp or sinking patch in the yard, driveway or planter, with no rain, points at a leak.

Meter turns with taps off

The meter shows water leaving your system. See leak detection.

Discolored or dirty water

Rust or silt after a pressure change can mean a corroded or broken line.

Where the City Stops and You Start

Meter, main and responsibility

The City of Burlingame buys its water from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission and delivers it through the public main and the meter. The line from the meter to your house is on your side.

If the leak is before the meter, or if the whole street has low pressure, call the city Water Division during business hours, or the city's after-hours dispatch for an urgent main break. If it is after the meter, call us.

Our plumber confirms which side the leak is on before recommending anything, so you do not pay to repair pipe that is not yours.

How a Line Is Repaired

Locate the line and the break

We trace the pipe route and use listening and pressure tests to find where it is failing.

Choose the repair method

A spot repair, a trench replacement or a trenchless option, depending on the pipe, depth and surface.

Repair and test

New pipe goes in, pressure is restored slowly and every joint is checked with the meter.

Restore the surface

We backfill and tell you what a gardener or paver will need to finish.

Where the surface above the line is a driveway or a mature planting, we price the approach that disturbs the least and explain why.

Replacement service piping must be rated for buried drinking water use under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and work in the public right-of-way needs a city permit.

Pressure, Pipe Age and Burlingame Elevation

Older Burlingame lines were often galvanized steel or early copper, and both corrode from inside. When a line has scale and pinholes, a repair at one spot is a short-term fix, and the next failure follows soon after.

Pressure plays a role too. The city runs booster pumps and pressure-reducing valves for areas above 120 feet in elevation, and a regulator on the house side protects your pipes from the high end of that range. We check it on every water line visit.

If the line is the weak point throughout the house, the answer may be a repiping plan, or a combined job with a burst pipe repair inside.
