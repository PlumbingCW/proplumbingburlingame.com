The neighborhood

About Easton Addition

The Easton name comes from Ansel Ives Easton, who bought the property from the Sanchez family in 1860. His son, Ansel Mills Easton, began subdividing it in 1905, and the town of Burlingame annexed Easton in 1910, according to a San Mateo Daily Journal history.

One real estate guide counts about 1,500 homes in the neighborhood and describes large, well maintained houses on tree-lined streets near Roosevelt Elementary School. Housing here is mixed, with older homes next to newer and rebuilt ones, so no single pipe type should be assumed.

Remodels, Additions and New Fixtures

Many homes in Easton Addition are updated, expanded or rebuilt, which brings a steady need for new bathrooms, kitchens and laundry hookups. Adding a bathroom means new drain routes, new supply lines and a hot water supply that can keep up.

Our plumbing installation work covers fixture swaps, rough-in for remodels and the connections behind them. Kitchens that gain a disposal can look at garbage disposal service, and faucet or valve trouble is handled through faucet repair.

Fixture Rules That Apply to Remodels

Burlingame Indoor Water Conservation Ordinance

For permitted new construction and kitchen and bath remodels, Burlingame sets toilets at 1.28 gallons per flush and showers at 2.0 gallons per minute. Bathroom faucets are limited to 1.5 gallons per minute and kitchen faucets to 2.2.

If you are planning a remodel in Easton Addition, choose fixtures with those ratings before you buy. The Building Division can confirm how the rule applies to your project.

Older Lines Under Newer Rooms

Where an older home has been remodeled, new fixtures can sit on much older drain and supply pipes. Cutting into a wall during a remodel is a good moment to look at what is already there.

The city maintains the sewer from the right-of-way cleanout to the main, and the pipe on the house side is the owner's. Buildings from before the mid-1960s were not required to have a cleanout, so a house that old may need a camera and a cleanout added before a big project.

A sewer line repair estimate starts with a look at the pipe, and we show you what we find before recommending work.
