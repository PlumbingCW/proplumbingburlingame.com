Planning a New Water Heater Install

A new install starts with the questions that decide everything else. How many people use hot water, how many bathrooms are there, where does the heater go and what fuel is available at that spot.

Answering those lets us size the tank or tankless unit correctly and avoid the two usual regrets, a heater that is too small to keep up and one that costs more than it should to run.

What Goes Into a Code-Compliant Install

  • A level base, with a drain pan where required.
  • Seismic strapping to meet California requirements under Health and Safety Code section 19211.
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge line to a safe location.
  • Proper venting and combustion air for gas units.
  • An expansion tank when the water system is closed.
  • Shutoff valve, flex connectors and a dielectric union where metals meet.
  • A permit and city inspection.

For a replacement of an existing unit, see water heater replacement.

Gas, Electric, Heat Pump or Tankless

Gas heaters heat quickly and need a vent and a gas line. Electric heaters have no vent and need a dedicated circuit. Heat pump models are efficient and need air space and electrical capacity, and Burlingame waives the permit fee when a heat pump replaces a gas heater or furnace.

Tankless units save space and supply endless hot water, and they need the right gas line and venting. Our tankless water heater page covers the details.

Bay Area air rules are shifting for gas units. The Air District delayed its zero-NOx standard to January 2028 in a 2026 vote, so ask us what applies to your purchase date.

Where the Heater Goes in a Burlingame Home

Garages are the usual spot in single-family houses, closets and utility rooms are common in condos and older homes, and some hillside houses have heaters in crawlspaces or under stairs. Each location changes the work, since a garage heater needs protection from vehicles and a closet unit needs combustion air and a drain pan.

Many Burlingame houses are older, and an older utility space may have been built around a smaller heater. We measure before we order, check clearances and plan the vent route and the relief line discharge, so the new unit does not run into a surprise on install day.

If a remodel or addition is adding the heater, we coordinate with your contractor on rough-in timing. See plumbing installation for the broader remodel work we handle.

Safety

Safety Notes for Gas Heaters

If you smell gas, leave the house, call 911 and then the gas utility. Do not use switches or phones inside. We install gas units with a shutoff and proper venting so carbon monoxide leaves the house, and we check combustion before we leave.
