Signs of a Hidden Leak That Should Not Wait

Water finds its way down through framing, insulation and subfloors, so the stain you see may sit far from the break. A leak that runs for weeks can grow mold, rot a sill plate and push a water bill up without any dripping you can hear.

Meter moves with everything off

Close every tap and appliance, then watch the dial. A moving dial or leak indicator means water is escaping somewhere.

Warm patch on the floor

A spot that feels warm underfoot can mean a hot water line is leaking in or under the slab.

Sound of running water at night

A faint hiss or trickle when the house is quiet is a pressurized line leaking.

Stains, bubbling paint or musty smell

Damp drywall, cupped flooring and a musty odor often trace back to a slow supply or drain leak.

If you can hear water or see it spreading, shut off the main valve and call. If you cannot find the valve, tell us on the phone and we will talk you through the usual places.

Checking the Meter and the High Bill

Start with the meter, since it is the quickest honest test. Turn off all water use, then read the meter and check again in 15 minutes. If the numbers change, a leak exists between the meter and your fixtures.

If the dial holds still but the bill climbed, look at habits and fixtures before you assume a hidden break. A running toilet flapper can waste a lot of water without a sound, and an irrigation timer stuck on can do the same outdoors.

Burlingame buys its water from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission, and the city reads the meter. If the meter checks out and the usage still looks wrong, the city Water Division can review the reading with you.

How We Pinpoint the Leak

Isolate the system

We shut off sections of the supply to learn whether the leak is on the pressurized side, the drain side or outdoors.

Listen and scan

Acoustic sensors hear water escaping a pressurized pipe, and a thermal camera shows temperature changes from wet or hot lines.

Pressure test

Gauges confirm which loop of pipe holds pressure and which drops, narrowing the search to one run.

Mark and explain

We mark the spot, show you the evidence and give you a price for the repair before opening anything.

This approach limits drywall and floor cutting to one small access hole rather than a long trench of guesswork.

Pressure testing is a recognized method in the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and it is how a plumber confirms that a repaired line holds.

Leaks Behind Walls, Under Slabs and Underground

Where the leak lives changes the repair. Wall and ceiling leaks usually mean opening a small section of drywall. Crawlspace leaks are reached from below, which saves your finished floors.

Slab and yard leaks are harder. Slab leaks sit in lines under the foundation and may be fixed by a spot repair, a reroute or a slab leak repair using the least invasive path. A leak in the line between the meter and the house is handled through water line repair.

Irrigation lines and pool plumbing leak too, and we check those when the numbers on the meter suggest water is leaving the house system. Once the leak is located, a failed section may need a burst pipe repair and older systems may justify repiping.

Pressure Is Part of the Story in Burlingame

Why we read your water pressure

Burlingame runs pressure-reducing valves and booster pump stations for areas above 120 feet in elevation, so pressure varies by neighborhood. A house on high pressure strains joints and washers and can develop pinhole leaks sooner.

We read pressure at the hose bib during a leak detection visit. If it runs high, a regulator is a small repair that protects every pipe in the house.
